## Project Overview

D6 Food Bulk is a student-focused platform that connects NSFAS-funded students with wholesalers. It aims to make essential food more affordable by providing student rates and allowing students to create customised food bulk packages according to their needs and budgets.

The current product scope serves students living in CPUT residences at the Cape Town Campus. This focused launch area allowed the team to work with a clearly defined user group while developing and validating the platform.

### Problem Statement

Students living in residence often need affordable food options but may not individually have access to wholesale pricing or food packages suited to their personal needs. D6 Food Bulk addresses this problem by bringing student-rate products, wholesalers, and customisable food bulks together on one platform.

### Product Vision

> To make food purchasing more affordable, flexible, and accessible for students by connecting them with wholesalers and enabling student-priced, customisable food bulks.

Our purpose was to design and develop D6 Food Bulk as an affordable food-purchasing solution for NSFAS students living in CPUT residences at the Cape Town Campus. We aimed to apply Agile practices so that the team could divide responsibilities, review progress regularly, respond to feedback, and deliver a usable product incrementally.

# 3. Product Backlog

The Product Backlog was the ordered source of work for D6 Food Bulk. It included user-facing functionality, technical work, quality improvements, and later enhancements. The team prioritised the core student journey before optional expansion.

## 3.1 Prioritisation Approach

We used a **MoSCoW-style approach** to distinguish essential work from valuable enhancements:

- **Must have:** required for the core student food-bulk journey;
- **Should have:** important but not essential for the first usable increment;
- **Could have:** beneficial once higher-value work is stable; and
- **Won't have yet:** deliberately deferred beyond the current scope.

When priorities competed, we considered user value, dependencies, risk, effort, and relevance to the current CPUT Cape Town Campus launch.

# Overall Learning

The D6 Food Bulk project helped me understand how Agile artefacts work together rather than existing as separate documents. The Team Charter establishes how the team intends to work; user stories describe valuable outcomes; the Product Backlog orders all known work; the Sprint Backlog creates a short-term delivery focus; and the Agile Logbook preserves decisions, evidence, and learning.

Most importantly, I learned that Agile is not simply moving cards across a board. It requires transparent communication, user-focused prioritisation, manageable work, reviewable outcomes, and regular reflection. Applying these principles helped connect our development activities to the real goal of providing students with more affordable and flexible food options.
