# Agile Logbook Reflection

## Situation

During the Entrepreneurial WIL project, our team developed D6 Food Bulk through a series of short sprints and Tuesday-to-Friday presentations. Between 21 July and 7 August 2026, the concept progressed from identifying the student problem to mapping the service process, refining user stories, coding the HTML frontend and researching deployment. We also had to manage uncertainty because supplier prices, delivery costs and delivery partners had not yet been confirmed.

## Task

My responsibility was to record my daily progress, planned work and impediments so that my contribution remained visible. I also needed to support the team by refining the business concept, updating user stories on Trello, preparing presentations, mapping the ordering and delivery process, contributing to frontend development and investigating a suitable hosting platform.

## Action

I recorded stand-up information for each reporting period and ensured that team activities were represented on the taskboard. I reviewed the student problem, target market and value proposition; helped prepare and present the business concept on Microsoft Teams; and mapped the Place and Pay, Pool and Fulfil, and Deliver and Collect processes. As the concept developed, I updated user stories, helped define student discounts, the Bulk Wallet, same-day residence delivery, Block Agent responsibilities and traceable handovers. During the August sprint, I began coding the frontend in HTML and researched hosting platforms for deployment. I also recorded blockers such as limited sprint time and unconfirmed supplier and transport costs.

## Result

The logbook shows how D6 Food Bulk developed incrementally from a general grocery-delivery idea into a more structured student-focused business and working digital product. The team clarified the core service process and identified features that addressed affordability, convenience and safe order collection. I learnt that Agile work requires more than completing tasks: it depends on visible work, clear ownership, focused stand-ups, early identification of blockers and regular refinement based on feedback. In future sprints, I would define acceptance criteria earlier, divide large activities into smaller tasks, assign owners and deadlines, and update the taskboard immediately when work changes.
