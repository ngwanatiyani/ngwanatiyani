## Sprint Review

### Progress made

Between 14 and 17 July, the project progressed from a general grocery delivery idea into a more structured business concept. The team identified the primary users, clarified the problems being addressed and developed a proposed process for placing, combining, fulfilling and delivering orders.

The sprint also produced clear features such as student discounts, same-day delivery, the Bulk Wallet, residence collection points, Block Agents and traceable order handovers.

### Challenges experienced

One of the main challenges was limiting the scope of the project. The team generated several ideas, but not all of them could be researched or developed during one sprint. Another challenge was making decisions without confirmed supplier and transport information. The team had to distinguish between confirmed project decisions and assumptions that still require validation.

Coordinating tasks and ensuring that every activity appeared on the taskboard was also an area that required improvement.

### Areas for improvement

In future sprints, the team should:

- Break large activities into smaller tasks.
- Assign an owner and deadline to every task.
- Update the task board immediately when work begins or ends.
- Define clear acceptance criteria for each user story.
- Validate prices and customer needs before finalising business decisions.
- Conduct shorter and more focused stand-up meetings.
- Record important decisions immediately.
- Avoid adding new features before completing the agreed sprint tasks.

### What I learnt

I learnt that Agile working is not only about completing tasks quickly. It also requires visibility, regular communication, prioritisation and continuous improvement.

The taskboard helped the team understand what was planned, what was being worked on and what had been completed. The stand-up questions made it easier to identify blockers before they caused major delays. I also learnt the importance of developing a solution from the user’s perspective. Writing user stories helped us connect each proposed feature to a real student need. The sprint demonstrated that a business concept becomes clearer when it is developed incrementally, reviewed regularly and adjusted using team feedback.
