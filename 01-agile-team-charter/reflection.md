# Agile Team Charter Reflection

## Situation

At the beginning of the D6 Food Bulk project, our team had to turn a shared business idea into a focused digital product. The platform needed to serve NSFAS students in CPUT residences while also presenting wholesaler products and student rates clearly. Because team members had different responsibilities, we needed a shared understanding of the project scope, expected quality and way of working.

## Task

My responsibility was to understand how my contribution supported the product vision and to work according to the team's agreed standards. I also needed to communicate my progress clearly, take ownership of assigned work and raise any issue that could delay the team.

## Action

I contributed to clarifying the purpose and initial scope of the platform, with the Cape Town Campus residences as the first service area. I used the shared Trello board to follow assigned work and make progress visible. I communicated blockers, considered feedback from other team members and checked that completed work supported the agreed student-focused outcome before treating it as finished.

## Result

The charter gave the team a common point of reference and made individual responsibilities easier to connect to the final product. It reduced uncertainty about our priorities and encouraged accountability. I learned that a team charter is useful only when its agreements guide daily communication, decision-making and quality throughout the project.
