# Sprint Backlog Reflection

## Situation

D6 Food Bulk was organised into five feature sprints that moved from student account access to the Product Catalogue, Shopping Cart, Bulk Ordering and Payment System. Each sprint contained a focused user story and Trello cards assigned to individual team members.

## Task

My responsibility was to work on and track the items assigned to me across Sprints 1–5. This included registration design and development, database design and integration, catalogue pricing and availability, cart design and persistence, bulk-order data and deadlines, payment-page work, gateway integration, payment history, testing, bug fixing and sprint reviews. The Shopping Cart page design was a shared team task.

## Action

I used the Trello sprint lists to keep my work linked to the relevant user story and sprint focus. For each sprint, I documented only the cards assigned to me and linked every item directly to Trello. I added one representative Trello screenshot per sprint and cross-referenced the related interface or database evidence. I retained the product-browsing test under Sprint 1 because that is where the current Trello board assigns it, rather than changing the sprint record in the portfolio.

## Result

The Sprint Backlog now provides traceability across five sprints through 21 assigned task links, five representative Trello screenshots and related portfolio evidence. It shows how my responsibilities progressed from account registration through data integration, cart and bulk-order support, and payment work. The records demonstrate task assignment and available evidence, but they do not independently confirm that every feature passed end-to-end testing or met a formal Definition of Done. I learnt that sprint reporting must accurately reflect the board while clearly separating assigned work, shared work and verified results.
