# Agile Logbook Reflection

## Situation

The D6 Food Bulk project involved planning discussions, Trello updates, development activities, reviews, blockers and changes in priority. These activities happened over time and across different channels, making it difficult to explain the development process accurately if evidence was collected only at the end.

## Task

My responsibility was to keep my contribution traceable by recording important activities, decisions, challenges and outcomes. I needed to connect these records to supporting evidence so that the logbook demonstrated both project progress and my learning.

## Action

I recorded key activities in chronological order and noted the purpose and outcome of each event. I used Trello activity, meeting information, screenshots and product outputs as supporting evidence where available. I also reflected on blockers and feedback instead of recording only successful work, because these experiences influenced later decisions and improvements.

## Result

The logbook provided a clearer account of how the project developed and how my work contributed to the final outcome. It made patterns in communication, planning and evidence collection easier to recognise. I learned that maintaining a logbook throughout the project creates a more reliable record and supports better reflection than trying to reconstruct events at the end.
