# Agile Logbook Reflection

## Situation

Between 14 July and 7 August 2026, the D6 Food Bulk concept developed through four recorded reporting periods. The team refined the student grocery problem, business idea and service process while managing limited sprint time and uncertainty about supplier prices, transport costs and delivery partners.

## Task

My responsibility was to record my daily progress, planned work and impediments while contributing to concept refinement, Trello updates, presentations, service-process mapping, initial frontend work and hosting research. The logbook needed to make both progress and unresolved risks visible.

## Action

I recorded updating user stories on Trello on 13 July before the first reporting period. Across the July entries, I reviewed the student problem, target market and value proposition, prepared and presented the business idea with the team on Microsoft Teams, and mapped the **Place and Pay**, **Pool and Fulfil**, and **Deliver and Collect** processes. The later July and August entries record that I started coding in HTML and researched hosting platforms. I also documented blockers, including scope pressure, limited time and unconfirmed supplier and transport costs, rather than presenting those assumptions as final decisions.

## Result

The four online logbooks and their original Word documents provide a traceable record from concept refinement and process mapping to initial frontend work and deployment research. They also identify improvements for future work, including smaller tasks, clearer owners and deadlines, earlier acceptance criteria, focused stand-ups and immediate taskboard updates. The records do not show a completed deployment or confirm the proposed pricing and delivery assumptions. I learnt that an Agile logbook is most useful when it records evidence, uncertainty and lessons honestly instead of only presenting positive progress.
